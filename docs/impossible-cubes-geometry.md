# Impossible Cubes: the geometry

*Impossible Cubes* flies through the regular honeycomb {4,3,5}, the hyperbolic space filled with cubes so that five of
them meet around every edge. In ordinary space only four cubes fit around an edge: each cube's faces meet at 90°, and
4 × 90° = 360°. In hyperbolic space a cube can be built with 72° between its faces, so five fit: 5 × 72° = 360°. The
shader draws that space exactly. No cube, beam or vanishing point is placed by hand. Every one of them follows from the
symmetry below.

Source: [`../works/impossible-cubes-435.frag`](../works/impossible-cubes-435.frag).

## The space

The shader works in the **hyperboloid model**.
- **The space.** Points of hyperbolic 3-space are vectors *p* in four dimensions with ⟨*p*, *p*⟩ = −1, where
  ⟨*x*, *y*⟩ = *x*₁*y*₁ + *x*₂*y*₂ + *x*₃*y*₃ − *x*₄*y*₄.
- **Rays.** A ray of light is a geodesic. One step of length *h* moves the point and its direction by
  *p* ← *p* cosh *h* + *d* sinh *h* and *d* ← *p* sinh *h* + *d* cosh *h*.
- **Precision.** Both are re-normalised after every step, so rounding never drifts off the hyperboloid.

## The symmetry

The honeycomb's symmetry group is the Coxeter group [4,3,5]. It is generated by reflections in four mirrors
*m*₀, …, *m*₃.
- **The mirrors.** Their unit normals satisfy ⟨*n*ᵢ, *n*ⱼ⟩ = −cos(π/*k*ᵢⱼ), with *k*₀₁ = 4, *k*₁₂ = 3, *k*₂₃ = 5 and
  every other pair at right angles.
- **The normals in closed form.** With *q* = cos 36° = (1 + √5)/4 and *t* = √(2*q*² − 1):

| Mirror | Normal |
| --- | --- |
| *m*₀ | (1, 0, 0, 0) |
| *m*₁ | (−√½, ½, −½, 0) |
| *m*₂ | (0, 0, 1, 0) |
| *m*₃ | (0, −*q*, −*q*, *t*) |

The four mirrors bound a small tetrahedron, the **fundamental simplex**, which contains one corner of each kind:

| Corner | On mirrors |
| --- | --- |
| The cube's centre *C* = (0, 0, 0, 1) | *m*₀, *m*₁, *m*₂ |
| The centre of a face, *F* | *m*₀, *m*₁, *m*₃ |
| The midpoint of an edge, *E* | *m*₀, *m*₂, *m*₃ |
| A vertex of the honeycomb, *V* | *m*₁, *m*₂, *m*₃ |

Reflected in every mirror again and again, this one tetrahedron fills the whole space, and the honeycomb with it.
- **Edges and faces.** Its edges lie on the line where *m*₂ and *m*₃ meet, and its faces lie in *m*₃.
- **The 72° angle.** It comes from *k*₂₃ = 5: the angle between *m*₂ and *m*₃ is π/5, so the dihedral angle of a cube is
  2π/5.

## Drawing it

Each pixel sends one ray and marches it as a **billiard ball** inside the simplex.
- **The march.** The ray takes 80 steps of length 0.1. After each step, if the point has crossed a mirror, both the
  point and the direction are reflected back.
- **Why it is cheap.** The ray never leaves the simplex, so the distance to the nearest edge, vertex or face is always
  a short local formula, however far the ray has travelled.
- **The light.**
  - **Beams.** Light gathers near the edge line: the distance is asinh √(⟨*p*, *a*⟩² + ⟨*p*, *b*⟩²), for an orthonormal
    pair *a*, *b* normal to the edge.
  - **Knots.** It gathers near the vertex: cosh of the distance is −⟨*p*, *V*⟩.
  - **Panes.** Faintly, it gathers near the face plane.
  - **Fog.** All of it fades with the distance *s* travelled, as exp(−*s*/2.5), so far cells dissolve into black.

## The flight

The camera flies from the cube's centre *C* through the face centre *F*.
- **The symmetry.** Moving along that line by twice the distance *d*(*C*, *F*) carries each cube's centre onto the next.
  The move combines two reflections of the group: one in the cube's own mid-plane, then one in the face plane. It is
  therefore a symmetry of the whole honeycomb.
- **The loop.** The shader wraps the flight at exactly that length, so it repeats seamlessly every 24 seconds.
- **The distance.** In closed form, *d*(*C*, *F*) = asinh *t* = atanh(*t* / (√2 *q*)) ≈ 0.5306, so one loop covers
  1.0613.

## How it was checked

The construction was recomputed independently, in double precision, from the constants in the shader:

| Check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The four normals against the Coxeter angles | All sixteen products match −cos(π/*k*ᵢⱼ) to machine precision |
| The vertex *V* in the shader | It lies on *m*₁, *m*₂ and *m*₃ and on the hyperboloid, inside the simplex |
| The dihedral angle of a cube | 72° |
| The edge length | 1.6169 |
| *d*(*C*, *F*) | 0.5306 from both closed forms; the loop is 1.0613 |

**Speed.** In the Studio's fullscreen view, with the code panel open, the Preview inspector reported 113 frames per
second, at 8.8 ms per frame. Rendered offline at 1920 × 1080 on the same laptop GPU, a frame took 8.9 ms.

**A known limit.** The shader takes one ray per pixel. The most distant edges are thinner than a pixel, so in motion
they twinkle. The animation in the README was rendered offline from the unchanged source at 4 × 4 samples per pixel,
which smooths that twinkle away. The still images come from the Studio's own fullscreen preview, at one sample per
pixel.
